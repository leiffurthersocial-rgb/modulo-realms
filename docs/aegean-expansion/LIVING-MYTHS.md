# Living Myths gameplay revision

## Design contract

The Greek expansion must feel like a new game region: every island has a recognizable ecology, landmark, hazard and activity, with mythology driving play as well as names. Challenge comes from aggression, positioning, attack combinations and environmental decisions. Normal bosses have at most 30,000 HP; the final island has separate budgets of 58,000 for champions and 110,000 for Leonidas. Increasing HP or hiding damage behind repetitive interactions is not difficulty.

Players should understand the next action while playing. Use short verbs, visible attack windups, map destinations, directional markers and exact reward receipts. The journal is optional reference. Keep progression, exploration, secrets, crafting and build depth; remove friction and prose dependency. Ship components need a real chart and one actionable next destination. Mechanisms should change the fight, with clear feedback and no arbitrary repeated three-button sequence.

## Implementation workstreams

- Bestiary: Greek-only field/sea rosters, island exclusives, per-species attack decks, physical attack patterns and bespoke boss silhouettes. Preserve the original world.
- Encounters: start on entry, active pursuit, short dangerous cycles, optional useful arena counterplay, compact staging. The Three Hundred is one simultaneous 300-soldier battle on earth, preserving earned save receipts.
- World: irregular routes and waterways, myth-specific island terrain, environmental hazards/weather, varied physical activities, working buildings and Asterion waystones.
- Guidance: shipwright component chart, persistent next-step tracking, real harbour/entrance navigation, concise HUD, explicit item/gold/XP/unlock receipts and storage overflow notices.
- Equipment/balance: distinct basic attacks and speed/reach identities for Greek weapons, existing activated powers retained; healing from offense uses actual damage; the September 21 follow-up restores full lifesteal without a per-hit or shared recovery cap.

## Implemented systems

- `ecology.ts` defines all 17 island rosters, mainland territories and five sea danger bands. The expansion has 54 native field species and 30 bespoke boss designs. Species have individual movement styles and ordered attack decks, including committed leaps, weapon sweeps, thrown nets, tail lashes, jaw grabs and aimed volleys.
- Nine environmental hazard families and seven weather effects give travel visible danger. Charybdis vortices warn before pulling ships; travelling squalls and nine native sea species punish careless crossings. Safe harbour/town/door areas remain clear.
- Bosses start automatically on entry. Ordinary arenas occupy roughly 60% of their previous dimensions, while attack counters are useful optional openings. Capture, rescue and traversal Labours retain their distinct completion rules with automatic proximity/impact handling and visible in-world cues. Normal boss HP is capped after scaling at 30,000; Asterion champions at 58,000 and Leonidas at 110,000.
- The Three Hundred are all present on one earth battlefield from the start. Spatial steering and five concurrent attack commitments bound simulation and warning noise; these are not reinforcement waves. Completed victories stay completed; unfinished old chapter saves restart the simultaneous battle.
- Thirty-one repeated ordered-button scenes use striking, timed traversal, holding ground or dodging instead. Escort/collection checkpoints advance from play. Failed escorts regroup before retrying, and a dead companion cannot count as delivered.
- Stormbreaker has a separate shipwright chart, numbered requirements, live prerequisite resolution and persistent guidance through departure harbours, real landings and Underworld doors. The journal is optional reference; quest text is generated from the actual adventure goals.
- Reward receipts name quantities, items, gold, XP and unlocks. The most recent receipt stays available from the HUD; full packs send important items to storage with an explicit message.
- Eighteen Greek weapons have distinct basic attack sequences, ranges and speeds while retaining their activated powers. Offense-driven healing uses actual damage and retains its full strength. The short-lived 2%-per-second/0.75%-per-hit cap was removed after player feedback. Immune hits and overkill cannot inflate healing. Passive recovery pauses under boss pressure; mending is bounded during Greek combat.
- Asterion has functional harbour/sanctuary waystones after the first voyage. Seven formerly decorative town-centre buildings are inhabited refuge halls with services. Existing doors, legacy world identities, original art and equipment progression are preserved.

## Future expansion preferences

Give each region a recognisable silhouette, ecology, atmosphere, local danger and meaningful activity. Invent mechanics and anatomy that belong to the setting; renaming or recolouring an original enemy is insufficient. Make difficulty come from aggression, spatial decisions and combinations, with accurate warnings and fair dodges. Keep encounter density moderate, apex creatures rare and sea travel navigable. Monsters must visibly throw, shoot, bite, strike or move; abstract filled damage fields are not an acceptable substitute. Preserve full-strength lifesteal from genuine damage. Prefer short, intense bosses to health sponges. Keep arenas purposeful and routes natural while preserving real collision connectivity.

Communicate the next action through the world, map and short verb cues. Show where unfamiliar names are, what blocks a purchase, what the player earned and where overflow went. Preserve secrets, crafting, build depth and progression; reduce reading and friction. Do not repeat the same three-interaction puzzle throughout a new map. Audit buildings and waypoints as functional play spaces, and test old saves as well as new games.

## Combat clarity and density follow-up — 21 September 2026

Player feedback corrects the initial balance direction: keep creative enemies and full-strength lifesteal, but reduce how many threats overlap. A successful hit heals its full configured percentage of actual damage, limited only by missing health; invulnerable hits and overkill still cannot manufacture recovery.

Greek enemy warnings now animate a weapon, limb, jaw or held projectile on a living nearby source. Damage follows moving weapon/body contact or the flight of a visible spear, arrow, boulder, feather, chain, net, ember or spit. Killing or leaving a source cancels its abandoned attacks. Arena counterplay follows actual projectile impacts, and interruptions visibly deflect incoming objects. Delayed player cuts follow the wielder, while quarry/hammer debris and flame bursts launch actual missiles; changing maps, dying or stowing the weapon cancels pending follow-ups. Activity pressure comes from a small local patrol; an empty scene cannot keep dealing anonymous damage after its defenders die.

On seed 1337, ordinary patrols fall from 1,710 to 1,244 (27% fewer) and their total creatures from 2,632 to 1,806 (31% fewer); rare creatures fall from 760 to 190. Surface patrols retain their original coordinate IDs and random-generation stream while a deterministic filter keeps 72% of patrol locations. Apex creatures receive a lower selection weight, appear singly and respawn after nine to eleven minutes. Existing native island territories and the full bestiary remain. Sea monster caps are 1/2/3/3/4 by danger band, with 25/21/17/13.5/10 seconds between attempts. The Three Hundred still start as 300 simultaneous soldiers; only four may commit attacks at once. Royal guards fall from six to four, with fewer secondary adds and more space between additional boss moves.

Ground traps are sparse, persistent objects: roots, thorn bushes, vents, loose rocks, statues, bronze rods, spear grates, broken water spouts and swinging bells. Their visible limbs or emitted objects define contact, and each must show the player at least 1.2 seconds of warning before it can hurt them. Naval storms show a low cloud and descending lightning instead of a filled circle. Whirlpools wait 110 seconds of actual open-water sailing before their first attempt, then 100–140 seconds between attempts; their pull and core damage are reduced. Map changes and time ashore cannot produce an immediate new whirlpool.

## Leonidas and Primordial overhaul — 21 September 2026

Asterion is the hardest combat destination. Each champion has two duels requiring three completed physical patterns each. Leonidas keeps six health phases but now requires completed attacks, two distinct oath braziers, all four royal guards, and a conductor discharge before the corresponding phase can end. The final three throws still resolve through real projectiles. Burst overkill cannot carry across phases, and an invulnerable phase floor cannot generate hit procs or lifesteal. On-screen objectives report the required actions and pattern counts. Attacks remain attached to nearby living sources with readable tells and ordinary dodges, brace, wards and shields.

Island champions and royal guards pursue faster and combine committed attacks. Leonidas's minimum damage starts at 18% of maximum health times the attack's actual power, capped at 55% before active defenses; his late unblocked sweep threatens about 54%. Ordinary mainland tuning is unchanged, apart from the Three Hundred's bounded five-attacker pressure on the approach. All healing consumables, including valley food and Grand Elixirs, share an 18-second recovery cooldown on Asterion. Keyboard and touch controls display the recovery timer. Actual-damage lifesteal remains full strength.

Primordial's base rarity power is 3.2 against Olympian's 1.52. Every royal weapon and sanctuary support receives stronger authored stats and six enchantment slots. Royal actives work on demand; successful braces, interrupts and alternating chain strikes empower the relevant attacks. Every First Flame combo beat retains a full aimed projectile, and both Oathblade cuts reach nearby targets. Greek attacks honor Committed, Swirling, Multishot, Chain Reaction and Echo. Inventory, storage and equipped Primordials refresh through the existing loader without changing their UID, provenance, rolled affixes or enchantments.

The desktop hotbar now exposes Greek weapon and artifact powers; item cards show their names, controls, cooldowns and descriptions. Primordial has its own starlight treatment and pickup celebration and sorts first within its equipment category. Above level 75, original equipment reforging now agrees with its existing reload curve instead of temporarily compounding damage by 11% per level until reload. Forging through level 75, prices, Crown behavior, and roll-only bonus fields keep their existing rules.

Regression coverage adds real consumable/pickup paths, forge-through-115/reload consistency, the complete rarity sort order, every Primordial versus lower-tier level-115 equipment, existing-item refresh idempotence, and live weapon/power behavior. Calibration reports damage time separately from mandatory phase mechanics; it does not claim a human completion time.

Validation: all 24 serial regression groups passed. After the final support-stat tuning, content, Primordial, damage and class-calibration checks were rerun and passed; `npm run build` passes with the existing bundle-size advisory. An isolated browser character confirmed six populated enchantment slots, the new item-card treatment, functioning V/R powers and cooldowns, and Leonidas's phase objective and strike counter. No page errors were reported. Browser inspection used invulnerability and is not a full human fight clear.
